I have a confession that will surprise nobody who collects stationery: I own more bottled ink than I can realistically use. Bottles arrive with the best intentions, get admired on the shelf, and then quietly go back to being furniture. A bottle holds far more ink than any sane person writes in a year, so the shelf keeps growing faster than it shrinks.

This year I finally found a system that is actually eating into the backlog, and it is embarrassingly simple.

## The system: one journal, many pens

Every night I journal in my **Hobonichi Five Year** techo — one page, one day, a single line of space per date. It is the perfect vehicle for ink rotation because each entry is small, forgiving, and permanent. Nobody grades it. The page just needs to be filled.

The rule is equally simple: each night, a different pen from a rotating lineup. No repeats until every pen in the current lineup has had its turn. With eleven pens in the rotation, a full cycle takes just under a fortnight. My current lineup, in no particular order:

1. [Kakimori Frost Fountain Pen – Graphite](https://www.kakimori.com/)
2. **Kaweco BRASS SPORT Fountain Pen**
3. **Pilot Vanishing Point Fountain Pen – Black Matte**
4. [Miskatonic University Fountain Pens](https://store.hplhs.org/products/miskatonic-university-fountain-pens)
5. **Pilot Kakuno Fountain Pen – Clear**
6. **Pilot Kakuno Fountain Pen – Panda** (limited edition)
7. **LAMY 2000 Fountain Pen – Black**
8. **Sailor Clear Candy Milk Fountain Pen – Milk Coffee**
9. **Pilot Custom 823**
10. **MUJI Aluminum Fountain Pen**
11. **Platinum 3776**

When the cycle completes, a pen gets cleaned and refilled from whichever bottle has been waiting longest. That is the whole trick. The lineup turns a shelf of ink into a queue, and the five-year journal turns the queue into a diary.

## Why the Five Year techo earns its keep

The Hobonichi's Tomoe River-style paper is the other half of the equation. This is paper that loves fountain pen ink: fine nibs stay crisp, wet writers barely feather, and shading inks do their best work here. A mediocre ink becomes interesting on this paper, and a great ink becomes the reason you look forward to bedtime.

Feeding the rotation right now are six bottles: **Diamine Midnight**, **Diamine Writer's Blood**, **Sheaffer Skrip Black**, and three from Pilot's Iroshizuku range — **Kon-Peki**, **Syo-Ro** and **Kiri-same**. Eleven pens sharing six bottles means some colours do double duty, which suits me fine: the point is emptying bottles, not achieving one-ink-per-pen purity.

There is also something fitting about the combination. A five-year journal is a long commitment, and rotating inks through it means each line carries a colour from a specific stretch of weeks. Flip back and the hue shifts — the bright blue stretch of **Kon-Peki** gives way to the deeper green of **Syo-Ro**, and I remember exactly which fortnight that was.

## The honest parts

Not everything has gone smoothly, and I would be lying if I said the system fixed everything:

- **Some inks are simply not worth the slot.** Swatching flattered a few bottles that daily writing does not. Most of my cartridge inks just don't *shine* on real pages, so it goes in the trade pile rather than the lineup.
- **Refilling pens is a chore.** A cycle end means cleaning, drying and refilling, which I keep putting off. My advice: refill the next morning with coffee, not at midnight with a headache.
- **Eleven pens is a lot of cleaning.** Any pen sitting a full cycle between uses gets a flush before it comes back into service, and that adds up.
- **The backlog shrinks slowly.** Even writing daily, a bottle lasts months. This is a multi-year project, and I have made peace with that.

## Verdict

A rotating pen lineup paired with a one-line-per-day journal is the best ink-reduction scheme I have tried, and unlike spreadsheets and discipline, it has actually lasted. The shelf is smaller than it was, the journal is filling, and every page is a small reminder of which pen and colour owned that day. If your ink collection has become decoration, give this a go — the queue does the deciding for you.
